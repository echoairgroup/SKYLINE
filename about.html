Where does the data come from?

Aircraft positions are supplied by ADSB.lol, a community-run network of independent ADS-B and Mode-S receivers. The provider aggregates radio messages and publishes a live map, public API and historical archive. Data availability depends on receiver coverage and what each aircraft broadcasts. ADSB.lol describes the public API and data as ODbL 1.0.

The embedded map and its aircraft interaction tools are served directly by ADSB.lol. SKYLINE does not copy or fabricate positions, routes, flight schedules or aircraft statistics.

Map data and software

The map is part of the ADSB.lol live radar interface. Its available base layers include OpenStreetMap-based maps and other provider-supported map layers. OpenStreetMap data is © OpenStreetMap contributors. Third-party map tiles have their own attribution and usage conditions.

ADSB.lol public data: Open Data Commons Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0. API documentation: api.adsb.lol/docs.

What SKYLINE does not claim

  • It does not promise to show every aircraft. Some aircraft are not equipped with ADS-B, do not transmit a usable position, are blocked or are outside receiver coverage.
  • ADS-B positions are not the same as an airline schedule. A callsign does not always identify the commercial route or destination.
  • Availability and update rates depend on the independent data provider, its receivers, network and API/map service.
  • This is an enthusiast tracking and information tool, not certified air traffic control, navigation, dispatch or flight-safety equipment.
